# Primary-source provenance and limits

## AQUAL and QUMOND

M. Milgrom, *Quasi-linear formulation of MOND*, MNRAS 403, 886–895 (2010), DOI: 10.1111/j.1365-2966.2009.16184.x. Pinned author manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.5464v2 (2 March 2010).

I. Banik and H. Zhao, *The External Field Dominated Solution in QUMOND & AQUAL: Application to Tidal Streams*. Pinned manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.08457v3 (4 April 2020); equations (18), (24), and (36)–(38). These supply the established external-field potentials. The residual comparison is calculated from them, with the observable and background pairing specified in the article.

M. Milgrom, *Models of modified-inertia formulation of MOND*, Physical Review D 106, 064060 (2022), DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.106.064060. Pinned author manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07073v3 (30 September 2022). This supports the model-dependent, trajectory-sensitive comparison; no universal residual for the modified-inertia class is asserted.

## Historical Yukawa benchmark

J. G. Lee, *A Fourier-Bessel Test of the Gravitational Inverse-Square Law*, University of Washington dissertation (2020), section 11.4 and Table 11.4, printed p. 134.

https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/bitstreams/6013936a-da00-42b7-ba0a-4dff0cb05bf8/download

| Range (micrometres) | Absolute-value 95% limit | Positive boundary at delta chi-squared = 6.17 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 2.08 | 2.56 |
| 35 | 1.45 | 1.77 |

The absolute-value limit integrates the fitted normal density between -a and +a to 0.95. The positive boundary uses delta chi-squared 6.17. These are distinct definitions. Both place the alpha=2 crossing between 32 and 35 micrometres, corresponding to L between 32 pi and 35 pi micrometres. On that branch, the lower endpoint is not excluded and the upper endpoint is excluded. The interval locates an exclusion edge; it is not an allowed window. No full-tower likelihood or strongest-current-bound claim is made.

## Light-deflection arithmetic

M. Partanen and J. Tulkki, *Light Deflection in Unified Gravity and Measurable Deviation from General Relativity in the Second Post-Newtonian Order*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14446v6 (16 September 2025), equations (26)–(28), printed p. 4. Coefficients multiplying pi at second order are 23/8, 11/4, and 15/4. They are model predictions with a common asymptotic-impact-parameter convention, not measured discrepancies and not predictions of the scalar examples.
